Why The Goat

We are a small British workshop that got tired of number plates being treated as an afterthought. Everything here follows from that.

A plate is the first thing anyone reads

It sits at eye level on the front of the thing you have probably spent more on than anything except your home. And for decades the standard experience of buying one has been a grainy product photo, a dropdown menu, and a lottery over what turns up in the post.

We built Caps Goat Plates to fix the two ends of that. The Plate Lab shows you the actual plate as you design it — front and rear, real depth, real proportions — so there is no guessing. And the bench work behind it is done properly: certified components, correct legal spacing, hand-placed characters, two inspections before anything is packed.

The goat is not decoration. Greatest Of All Time is the standard we hold ourselves to on a product most people assume cannot be done well.

The Promise

The GOAT Guarantee.

Most plate makers offer three years. We could not work out why a plate should be allowed to fail in year four.

Lifetime cover

If a character peels, cracks, lifts or fades, we remake and reship the plate free of charge, for as long as you own it. No pro-rata, no admin fee.

Right-first-time

If we press something that does not match your build, that is on us — we remake it immediately and cover the postage both ways.

Straight answers

We will tell you when a plate you want is not road legal, even when it costs us the sale. That is the whole basis of being a registered supplier.

Standards

What We Will Not Do.

Sell illegal plates as legal

Tinted, misspaced and custom-font plates are sold here as show plates, marked as show plates, and described honestly. Always.

Skip the paperwork

No documents, no road-legal plate. A supplier who waives that is not doing you a favour — they are exposing you.

Use uncertified blanks

Non-standard blanks are cheaper and fail MOTs. We do not keep them in the building.

Hide the price

Every option in the Lab shows its cost at the moment you add it. The total on screen is the total you pay.
