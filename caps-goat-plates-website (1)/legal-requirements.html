Legal & Documents

What UK law requires from a number plate, what it requires from you, and exactly which documents we need before we can press a road-legal order.

The two documents we need

Every registered number plate supplier in the UK is legally required to see two things before making a road-legal plate. This is not our policy — it applies to every legitimate supplier in the country, and anyone skipping it is not operating legally.

1. Proof of identity

Any one of the following, showing your name and address:

  • UK photocard driving licence
  • Passport, together with a utility bill or bank statement from the last three months
  • National identity card, with the same supporting document

2. Proof of entitlement to the registration

Any one of the following, showing the registration you are ordering:

  • V5C vehicle registration certificate (the log book)
  • V5C/2 new keeper supplement
  • V750 certificate of entitlement
  • V778 retention document
  • A dated authorisation letter from the registered keeper, plus their documents

Photographs are fine as long as every corner is visible and the text is readable. We check by hand, usually within the hour during working days.

What makes a plate road legal

A plate fitted to a vehicle on a public road must:

  • Be made from a reflective material meeting the current British Standard (BS AU 145e for plates supplied since September 2021)
  • Display black characters only — a single, solid shade, with no shading, patterning or colour
  • Use the mandatory typeface at the prescribed character height, width, stroke and spacing
  • Have no background pattern — white to the front, yellow to the rear
  • Show the supplier’s name and postcode and the British Standard number
  • Be kept clean and legible — a dirty plate is an offence in itself

Permitted badges

You may display one national identifier on the left of the plate: the Union flag with UK or GB, the Cross of St George with ENG, the Saltire with SCO, or the Red Dragon with CYM. Green flashes are permitted on zero-emission vehicles. Anything else — club logos, foreign flags, slogans — makes the plate a show plate.

What it costs to get it wrong

Displaying a non-compliant plate on a public road carries a fine of up to £1,000. Your vehicle will fail its MOT. Where the plate has been altered to misrepresent the registration, the DVLA can also withdraw your right to that registration permanently — which, on a valuable private plate, is a considerably more expensive outcome than the fine.

Buying a plate for someone else

You can, but we still need the registered keeper’s proof of entitlement along with a signed, dated authorisation letter and your own ID. Gift orders are common and we are happy to arrange them discreetly — just tell us in advance so we can handle the paperwork without spoiling the surprise.
