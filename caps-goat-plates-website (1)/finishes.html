There is no such thing as an illegal 4D plate by definition. A plate becomes illegal through what you do to it — coloured characters, altered spacing, tinted backgrounds, non-standard fonts. On The Bench

How We Build Them.

1

Certified blank

We start with a BS AU 145e reflective blank — the same standard the DVLA and MOT testers expect. Cheap plates fail here first, and no amount of finishing rescues a bad blank.

2

Legal layout

Characters are set in the mandatory typeface at the correct height, width, stroke and spacing for your registration. This is done to spec, not by eye.

3

Print, cut or cast

Standard plates are thermally printed. 3D gel characters are cast in resin and domed. 4D characters are laser-cut from 3 mm acrylic and placed by hand, one at a time.

4

Bond, cure and inspect

Everything is bonded with automotive-grade adhesive, cured, then checked against your build sheet under a bright light before packing.

One minute. One plate. Yours.
